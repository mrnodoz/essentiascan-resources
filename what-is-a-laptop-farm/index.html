What is a laptop farm? A plain guide for hiring teams

2026-11-20 · Sources linked below

How remote IT worker schemes keep company laptops running from someone's home, what federal investigators have found, and what the FBI tells employers.

The short answer

A laptop farm is a home where someone keeps company-issued laptops switched on and connected, so a remote worker somewhere else can log in and appear to be working from inside the United States.

The FBI describes it this way:

“ITWs pay facilitators to keep employment-related devices connected to the Internet from non-alerting locations, such as personal residences, while ITWs remotely access the devices to perform work. These arrangements, often called "laptop farms", have allowed ITWs to obfuscate their locations for years.”FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center, "North Korean IT Workers Expanding Use of Facilitators, Broadening Recruitment", October 13, 2026 [1]

"ITWs" is the FBI's short name for North Korean IT workers.

How big is this?

This is a national problem. Federal cases from the past two years involve employers and identities across the United States:

  • In one set of schemes, the Justice Department said North Korean workers “successfully obtained employment with more than 100 U.S. companies” [3]
  • In another case, the Justice Department said “The multi-year scheme used the stolen identities of at least 80 U.S. persons” [4]
  • In June 2025, the Justice Department said “the FBI executed searches of 21 premises across 14 states hosting known and suspected laptop farms” [3]
  • And in one state: in a 2026 operation in Texas, the FBI reported “executing operations at more than 40 suspected ‘laptop farms’ across Texas” [2]

Why one check is not enough

The FBI's October 2026 guidance is direct about this:

“ITWs cannot be identified reliably with a single indicator”FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center, "North Korean IT Workers Expanding Use of Facilitators, Broadening Recruitment", October 13, 2026 [1]

Instead, the FBI describes an approach it calls "identity in depth": layered checks across the whole employment lifecycle, not a single gate at the start. Among the steps it lists, it says:

“Using E-Verify, requiring a U.S. passport, multiple forms of identification, or live verification can raise friction for ITWs.”FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center, "North Korean IT Workers Expanding Use of Facilitators, Broadening Recruitment", October 13, 2026 [1]

What hiring teams can do this week

  • Read the FBI's October 2026 announcement in full (linked below). It lists the behaviors it has seen and its recommendations for companies.
  • Look at your process from the first conversation through the first day, not only at the interview.
  • If you think you have met this activity, the FBI asks you to contact your local FBI field office and file a complaint with the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3).
